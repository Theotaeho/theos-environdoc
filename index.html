Hi! My name is Taeho Kim. I am an ecohydrology researcher working to observe, understand, and diagnose the Earth system.

I use field observations to capture how environmental systems actually behave, process understanding to better represent the mechanisms that shape those systems, and Earth system modeling to investigate how they may evolve under a changing climate. By connecting Field to Earth, I aim to develop a more complete understanding of how the Earth system works and changes.

Let’s make big waves together.

01

Research Interests

Ecohydrology

How water moves, is stored, and is partitioned across the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum, and the biophysical mechanisms that regulate these exchanges. Field-based process understanding provides the basis for mechanistic model representations that better capture ecosystem water dynamics across scales and contribute to more realistic representations of the Earth system.

Hydroclimatic Extremes

Identify emerging hydroclimatic extreme phenomena and characterize their frequency, duration, intensity, and drivers. Quantify ecosystem responses and their impacts on water and carbon cycles, improve model representations of these processes, and scale observed patterns from local ecosystems to regional and global scales.

Ecosystem Resilience

How ecosystem stability and resilience vary across vegetation and hydroclimatic gradients, which environmental and biological factors drive ecosystem vulnerability, where and why vulnerability hotspots emerge, and how these patterns may shift under future environmental change.
