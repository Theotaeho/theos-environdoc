CV

I am a PhD candidate in Environmental Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Valeriy Ivanov. My work links direct field observation of ecosystem processes to more faithful representations of the Earth system.

I work where measurements meet models. In the Arctic forest–tundra ecotone and the Amazon rainforest I operate monitoring systems that record how water and energy move through the subsurface–plant–atmosphere continuum. Those records expose phenomena that models do not yet resolve, and they are the evidence I use to correct, extend and test process-based representations of vegetation and hydrology.

My research began with environmental big data and statistical learning. I grew more interested in the mechanisms behind the predictions than in the predictions themselves, which led me to field observation and physics-based modeling — not as replacements for machine learning, but as the complement that makes it explanatory.

Research Interests

Ecohydrology

Water and carbon exchange across the subsurface–plant–atmosphere continuum: plant water-use strategies, water history and residence time, and how both are represented in integrated Earth system models.

Hydroclimatic Extremes

Extreme evapotranspiration as an ecohydrological shock — its frequency, duration, intensity and severity — and the rapid ecosystem water loss and flash drought that can follow it.

Ecosystem Resilience

How stability and recovery vary across vegetation and hydroclimatic gradients, where rising vapor pressure deficit makes ecosystems vulnerable, and how vulnerability hotspots emerge.
