Themes & Field Sites

Research

Three lines of work, each beginning at a measurement site and ending in a representation that an Earth system model can carry.

01

Arctic — Radiation Correction

Forest–tundra ecotone, Ural Mountains · Sep. 2022 – present

Radiation measurements in high latitudes are quietly corrupted by precipitation: water and snow on the sensor dome bias the recorded flux. I developed a correction framework for these precipitation-induced biases across the Arctic forest–tundra ecotone, then used the corrected record to analyze heterogeneous net radiation patterns between forest and tundra. The corrected picture shows how Arctic greening can amplify warming through land–atmosphere feedbacks — a signal that the uncorrected observations understate.

Published in Agricultural and Forest Meteorology (2025); presented at AGU 2023.

02

Amazon — Sapflow

Santarém, K67, Brazil · Aug. 2023, May & Aug. 2024, Aug. 2025

Trees do not all drink the same way. Working from sapflow measurements in the Amazon rainforest, I categorized diurnal sapflow patterns as sapflow functional types and built a tree-trait-based framework for upscaling water-use strategies into Earth system models. Alongside this I constructed a predictive framework for sapflow measurement with causal interpretation, quantifying how drought and forest structure shape tree water use across timescales, and I am developing integrated ecohydrological models (ATS and tRIBS+VEGGIE) for the same landscape.

In review at New Phytologist (co-first author); presented at AGU 2024 and AGU 2025.

03

EcoLPT

Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Water Systems Modeling Group · Aug. 2023, Jun.–Jul. 2024, Jan. 2026 – present

Eulerian models tell you the state of water at a place. They do not tell you where that water came from or how long it has been there. EcoLPT is a Lagrangian particle tracking framework for ecohydrology that reconstructs water history across a landscape: it follows water from precipitation through coupled surface and subsurface pathways to evapotranspiration or watershed export, quantifying origin, trajectory, residence time and path length.

Using field observations from the Amazon, I built a physics-based watershed simulation to resolve Eulerian states and fluxes, then applied EcoLPT to reconstruct the corresponding Lagrangian histories. The combined view exposes water connectivity, transport pathways, storage selection and hydrologic memory that states and fluxes alone cannot show.

Manuscript in preparation for Geoscientific Model Development.

04

Methods — Learning for Environmental Systems

A parallel line of methodological work addresses complex structure, incomplete observation and computational cost. I have used Bayesian inference and network-based methods to represent hierarchical relationships, applied genetic algorithms to optimize air-quality monitoring networks, and developed an interpretable AI framework that reconstructs complete time series from incomplete inputs. More recently I have worked on neural-operator approaches that cut the computational cost of physics-based models while improving uncertainty quantification, transferability and generalization in natural-hazard prediction.
