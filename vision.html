Field-to-Earth Ecohydrology

Link direct observations of ecosystem processes to faithful representations of the Earth system

📶Field

Earth-system signals captured through field measurements and remote sensing provide the evidence needed to diagnose current ecosystem conditions, identify emerging stresses, and distinguish transient variability from persistent change. These observations also reveal previously unresolved phenomena and provide the empirical foundation for understanding how environmental systems respond to a changing climate.

💧🌿Process

Observations become scientifically meaningful when the mechanisms underlying observed patterns can be identified and quantified. Process-based analysis connects environmental signals to the physical, hydrological, and biological interactions that regulate water, energy, and carbon exchanges across the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum.

Mechanistic and diagnostic frameworks further allow complex ecosystem responses to be characterized in terms of their drivers, timescales, pathways, and consequences. By resolving how water is stored, transported, exchanged, and ultimately released, these approaches translate observations into transferable process understanding that can be represented across ecosystems and spatial scales.

🌍Earth

Mechanistic understanding provides the foundation for scaling local observations and processes to regional and global Earth-system behavior. Integrating observations, process-based models, remote sensing, and data-driven approaches can improve representations of vegetation–hydrology interactions, hydroclimatic extremes, and ecosystem responses to environmental change.

These advances strengthen predictions of future ecosystem trajectories and help identify where and when vulnerability may emerge under changing climate and land conditions. The ultimate goal is not simply to improve models, but to translate Earth-system understanding into actionable knowledge for anticipating environmental risks, identifying vulnerable regions, and supporting sustainable water, ecosystem, and land management.
